What is HP Build Workspace

HP Build Workspace is a cloud platform that helps construction teams manage drawings — keeping everyone aligned on the latest version, from the architect's office to the construction site. It connects the people who design the plans with the teams responsible for executing them.

My role

I led the research, use case mapping, and experience design and strategy for the drawings-related value stream, working closely with the Product Manager, engineering and quality team.

No clear version control, no clear communication between site and office

No clear version control

Without a clear way to track what's approved, teams can end up building from the wrong version — a mistake that costs real money to undo.

Site issues don't reach the office

When something needs fixing on site, there's no clear path for that issue to become the next, corrected version.

This case study focuses on the first moment of that gap — opening a drawing and knowing what you're looking at. The full path from a site issue to the version that resolves it is still in progress.

Making the drawing the place where communication happens

HP Build's value proposition is that the drawing is the single source of truth connecting site and office. For that to work, every job around a drawing — checking a version, flagging an issue — has to happen on the drawing itself, not scattered across WhatsApp or email.

I mapped the JTBD (jobs to be done) for each persona, based on 22 interviews and shadowing sessions in Barcelona. The clearest pattern: checking the drawing and its activity, or logging a new issue, happened far more than anything else.

Version status, always visible. Version info used to live as a hyperlink in the header — easy to miss. We considered making version history the default view, but JTBD research showed people ask "what's the current state of this version?" far more often than "what changed between versions?". So the badge leads with the active version and its status; history is one click away, not the default.

Activity, the main view. This was a new capability. Comments used to be plain text — no way to pin them to a spot on the drawing, no status, just a comment. Activity makes issues trackable: a comment, markup or document tied to a location and a state, so anyone can see not just what was said, but what's still open and needs to become the next version.

The tools people actually use, always visible. These used to sit behind a mode you had to turn on, with no real distinction between them. Mapping the JTBD showed they're the same underlying job — flag something at a specific point on the drawing — just in different formats for different ways of working. So we designed three explicit tools, always visible, each person reaching for the trigger that fits.

The QR code, at the moment it's actually needed. Instead of sitting in the main toolbar competing with daily actions, the QR toggle now lives inside the print/download modal, the exact moment someone is producing a physical copy and needs it.

HP Build now connects what's happening on site and office, in one place, without anyone needing to double-check elsewhere.

Three metrics, each answering a different question: are people using it, can they complete the core tasks, and how does it feel to do it.

72% of active projects with version control adopted.
92% success rate of key JTBD tasks.
