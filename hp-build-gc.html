What is HP Build Workspace

HP Build Workspace is a cloud platform that helps construction teams manage drawings — keeping everyone aligned on the latest version, from the architect's office to the construction site. It connects the people who design the plans with the teams responsible for executing them.

My role

I led the research, use case mapping, and experience design and strategy for the drawings-related value stream, working closely with the Product Manager.

No clear way to know what's approved, and no clear path from a problem on site to an updated drawing

No clear version control

Without a clear way to track what's approved, teams can end up building from the wrong version — a mistake that can cost real money to undo.

Site issues don't reach the office

When something needs fixing on site, there's no clear path for that issue to become the next, corrected version.

What this case study is about is narrower: the moment someone opens a drawing to take a quick look. At that moment, today, you can't tell if you're looking at the current version or an old one, or whether there's an open comment waiting on you. That small moment of confusion is what the redesign below addresses. The bigger question — connecting a specific field issue all the way to the version that resolves it — is still open. Making the drawing the place where communication happens

HP Build's value proposition is that the drawing is the single source of truth connecting site and office. For that to be true, every job people do around a drawing, checking a version, flagging an issue on site, has to happen on the drawing itself, not scattered across WhatsApp or email.

I mapped the JTBD (jobs to be done) for each persona around a drawing, based on 22 interviews and shadowing sessions in Barcelona. Mapping those jobs against frequency per persona shaped the information architecture and the flows below.

Version status, always visible. A badge and version selector now sit at the top. All actions related to version control are grouped under the version identifier, an easy way to navigate and understand what changed between versions.

Activity, the main view. An easy way to understand what's happening on site, and what needs to be updated in the drawing, which will generate a new version.

The tools people actually use, always visible. Draw, Comment, Pin: different triggers and formats (images, comments, markups) for the same underlying job, communicating something about a specific area or element of the drawing, adapted to each person's needs and way of working.

The QR code, at the moment it's actually needed. Instead of sitting in the main toolbar competing with daily actions, the QR toggle now lives inside the print/download modal, the exact moment someone is producing a physical copy and needs it.

Outcome

The drawing now connects what's happening on site and office, in one place, without anyone needing to double-check elsewhere.

Three metrics, each answering a different question: are people using it, can they complete the core tasks, and how does it feel to do it.
